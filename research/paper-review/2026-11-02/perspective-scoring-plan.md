## Contract Paraphrase

D1 (methodology_rigor): The design of the study, the handling of its data, how its statistics are reported, and what it gives readers to reproduce the work must clear the bar a careful referee in the home field would set. From a cross-disciplinary seat, I note that rigor here also decides whether a reader from a neighbouring field can trust the evidence without having to re-derive the field's unstated conventions. This dimension is scored by the methodology seat, not by me.

D2 (domain_accuracy): Claims must match the current state of evidence in the field, earlier work must be described fairly and correctly, and domain terms and reported results must contain no factual errors. Read across disciplines, accurate terminology matters twice: a misused term misleads specialists and also gives adjacent-field readers a wrong mental model they cannot easily correct. This dimension is scored by the domain seat.

D3 (argumentative_coherence): The central thesis must hold together, the evidence offered must actually support the claims made, and no reasoning error may undermine the main argument. For readers coming from other fields, coherence is what lets the argument travel: a chain of reasoning that depends on a hidden in-field premise reads as a gap to outsiders. This dimension is scored by the devil's advocate and methodology seats.

D4 (cross_disciplinary_relevance): The framing, the definitions and the stated implications must be understandable to readers in neighbouring fields, and any claim that reaches into another discipline must be backed by evidence or by sound engagement with that discipline. This is the dimension I own: I check whether a competent outsider can follow the problem, the vocabulary and the consequences, and whether statements about other fields' practice, threat models or needs are supported rather than asserted.

D5 (writing_and_structure): The manuscript must be well organised, clearly explained, have figures and tables of good quality, and follow the venue's conventions. From my seat, structure is the main thing that gives adjacent-field readers a way in, through signposting, early definitions and a findable statement of scope. This dimension is scored by the editor-in-chief seat.

D6 (venue_fit_and_contribution): The manuscript must suit the configured venue and offer a contribution that is original and significant for that venue's readers. Across disciplines, significance includes whether the contribution is useful beyond its home community, but judging fit and contribution belongs to the editor-in-chief seat, not to me.

## Scoring Plan

### D4: cross_disciplinary_relevance

dimension_id: D4
what_to_look_for: Whether core terms are defined at first use in language an adjacent-field reader can follow; whether the problem framing and threat or trust assumptions are stated explicitly instead of inherited silently from the home field; whether the implications for neighbouring communities (practitioners, policy, adjacent research areas) are spelled out and scoped; and whether every claim about another discipline's methods, needs or results is supported by citation, evidence or argument.
what_triggers_block: A central interdisciplinary claim, one the paper's stated contribution or implications depend on, is asserted without supporting evidence or citation, or misrepresents the other field, so that an adjacent-field reader acting on it would be misled.
what_triggers_warn: Framing or definitions are accessible only with home-field background (undefined jargon, implicit assumptions, missing scope statements), or secondary cross-field implications are overstated or weakly supported, but the central argument stays sound and the gaps can be fixed in a revision.
