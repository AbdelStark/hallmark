## Contract Paraphrase

D1 (methodology_rigor): As the adversarial seat I read this as asking whether the study's design, its data handling, the way it reports numbers, and what a reader gets to reproduce it would hold up against a hostile replicator. I would look for places where a hidden choice, an undisclosed parameter, or a missing artifact would let a skeptic dismiss the results. The methodology seat scores it; I only feed challenges through D3 where a methodological gap breaks the argument.

D2 (domain_accuracy): This dimension asks whether every claim is consistent with what the field currently accepts, whether prior work is described fairly rather than as a straw man, and whether technical terms and reported results are used correctly. From the adversarial side the risk is selective citation, a mischaracterized competitor, or a term used loosely so that it quietly widens a claim. The domain seat scores it.

D3 (argumentative_coherence): This is my dimension. It asks whether the central thesis holds together from start to finish, whether each conclusion is actually carried by the evidence offered for it, and whether any reasoning error, such as circularity, equivocation, false dichotomy, unstated assumption, or overreach from a narrow result to a broad claim, undermines the main argument. My job is to construct the strongest counter-argument and check whether the manuscript survives it.

D4 (cross_disciplinary_relevance): This asks whether a reader from a neighbouring field could follow the framing and definitions and see why the implications matter to them, and whether any claim that crosses field boundaries is backed rather than asserted. Adversarially, I would watch for borrowed vocabulary used with a different meaning than its home field gives it. The perspective seat scores it.

D5 (writing_and_structure): This covers how the manuscript is organised, how clearly it explains itself, whether figures and tables carry their weight, and whether it follows the venue's conventions. The adversarial angle is whether structure hides a weak link, for example a key caveat buried far from the claim it qualifies. The editor seat scores it.

D6 (venue_fit_and_contribution): This asks whether the work belongs at the configured venue and whether it adds something new and significant for that readership rather than repackaging known results. Adversarially, I would test whether the claimed novelty survives once the contribution is stated in its plainest form. The editor seat scores it.

## Scoring Plan

### D3: argumentative_coherence
dimension_id: D3
what_to_look_for: Trace each headline claim back to the specific evidence or derivation offered for it; state the strongest counter-position and check whether the text addresses it; check that key terms keep one meaning throughout; list the unstated assumptions each conclusion needs and whether the stated threat model or scope actually grants them; flag any step where a narrow result is generalised to a broader conclusion.
what_triggers_block: A headline conclusion depends on an unstated or unjustified assumption, or on a term whose meaning shifts between sections, such that a reasonable counter-argument defeats the claim as written and the manuscript does not rebut it, though a scoped restatement or added argument could repair it.
what_triggers_warn: A secondary claim is stated more broadly than its evidence supports, a plausible counter-argument is left unaddressed without defeating the main thesis, or a caveat that limits a conclusion is present but separated from the claim it qualifies.
what_triggers_fatal: The central thesis is internally contradictory, circular, or directly refuted by the manuscript's own evidence or definitions, so that no rescoping short of changing the core claim would make the argument hold.
